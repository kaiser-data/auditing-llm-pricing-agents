About this demo

What an audit looks for. One way to test a pricing agent for collusion is to replay the market with and without a rival price cut and ask: does the agent keep its own price lower for several periods afterwards? A lasting response looks like punishment, the mechanism that holds tacit collusion together.

Execution rules. Many markets put rules between a price decision and the price customers see. Germany's 2026 fuel-price “noon rule” allows increases only at 12:00 while cuts are allowed at any time; Western Australia's FuelWatch locks each price for 24 hours; platforms can hold an offer for a set time. Here “Hold” and “Noon rule” are simplified versions of these.

What the demo shows. A myopic agent that only best-responds to today's rival price returns to normal right away. Behind a hold, its published price stays down anyway, so an auditor reading published prices sees what looks like punishment. Comparing proposed with published prices separates the rule's effect from the agent's. With a real LLM (Qwen3-235B), the rule also changes the proposals, because the model anchors on its own held-down price.

Limits. Synthetic linear market, scripted rival, one model, small samples. This demo does not show that any firm or model colludes, and it is not legal advice.
